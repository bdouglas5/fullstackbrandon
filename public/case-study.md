# Fullstack Brandon — a world you can inspect

By Brandon Douglas.

I built a miniature 3D world around a business with real simulation constraints. A founder begins on foot with zero coins and six prepaid cases in a home garage. Every delivery changes inventory. Earned money pays for imported supplies, construction, equipment, employees, and their individual transport. Later, a negotiated factory island grows, ferments, and packs local produce.

Visitors can watch the career, influence priorities, queue investments, follow a courier, introduce disruptions, and inspect decisions. The browser uses React and procedural Three.js geometry. Node and Express own the API and simulation coordination. SQLite preserves guest worlds, replay frames, checkpoints, achievement collections, and provider usage.

Jev selects among explicit legal actions. The engine owns arithmetic, eligibility, routes, and consequences. Missing configuration, timeouts, invalid responses, uncertainty, and allowance limits use a labeled deterministic fallback. Saved lessons provide bounded operating context; they do not retrain the model.

The world connects its visuals to those rules: shipments arrive at a harbor, couriers carry actual stock, vehicles have ownership and footprints, handoffs happen at destinations, and weather belongs to the saved run. Replay uses recorded states without repeating model requests. Branches explore a substituted legal action from a retained checkpoint.

I enjoy building systems whose behavior you can explore and understand. This one happens to include rocket skates, a teleportation budget, and an entrepreneur learning that weekends exist.

The source includes tests, deterministic evaluation tooling, CI, backups, and a persistent-storage deployment configuration. This is a single-process simulation of an invented business. Public hosting, physical-device performance, and human playtest acceptance require separate checks.
